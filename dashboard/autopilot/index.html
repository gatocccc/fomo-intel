    A night at the tavern

    Five steps, from the door to the bar and back out with your money. You never pick tokens, timeframes or sizes. That is Lloyd's job.

    1. Make an account

      An email and a password. No app to install, no chat group to join.

    2. Get your own wallet

      One bot wallet per person, however many ducks you hold. Lloyd can swap inside it, and nothing else. It cannot withdraw.

    3. Fund it, set three dials

      How much goes in, the biggest drop you are willing to see, and what happens with profits. That is the whole form.

    4. Lloyd works four pockets

      Cash, majors, established memes and small picks. He shifts money between them as the market changes, and sits in USDC when nothing is good enough.

    5. Watch, withdraw, or close

      Every move shows up in your dashboard with its reasoning. Withdraw to your own wallet whenever you like, or hit "close everything" and it all goes to USDC.

    The four pockets

    Lloyd's book is split into four pockets. The split is not fixed: it follows the market, and you can see it live on the bar above. Your money mirrors the same split, scaled to your risk dial.

    Pocket one

    Cash

    USDC. The default pocket and never a punishment: it is what Lloyd holds when nothing passes his filters, and where everything goes when you close.

    In a falling market this pocket gets big on purpose. Not trading is a trade.

    Pocket two

    Majors

    SOL, BTC, ETH and JUP, bought when they trade above their 20-day average and sold when they drop back below it. Slow, boring, and the first thing Lloyd buys when the room turns constructive.

    Coming next: shorting them when they fall, through perps.

    Pocket three

    Established memes

    Mid and large memecoins with real liquidity: BONK, WIF, SPX, USELESS and company. Bought when they get back into an uptrend, sold when the trend breaks. No guessing bottoms.

    Pocket four

    Picks

    Small memecoins, roughly US$300K to US$30M, with a story or a catalyst. Bought on a pullback, never on the green candle, and only after the anti-rug check: who funded the top holders, whether the creator is a token factory, whether bundlers are in.

    Stops only ever go up. Partial profit taken at 3x and 6x. Dead money gets rotated out.

    How the split moves

    Twice a day, and after any sharp move, Lloyd reads the room from SOL and BTC against their 20-day averages and how many majors sit above theirs. Three moods, three allocations.

    • ConstructiveRisk on. Most of the book is working: majors, established memes and picks all get their budget. Cash is what is left over.
    • NeutralSelective. Majors above trend are held, picks need a stronger story, and a bigger share stays in USDC waiting for a better hand.
    • DefensiveRisk off: SOL is falling hard. Mostly cash, no new picks, trends that break get sold. Once majors shorts ship, this is where they live.

    House rules

    Most of us lose for the same reasons: we fall for a token, we move the stop, we trade to get a bad day back, and we never check who is behind it. Lloyd skips all of them.

    It checks who is behind it

    Before any pick, it traces who funded the top holders, whether the creator is a token factory, whether bundlers are in, and whether it can actually sell. If it smells like a rug, it stays out.

    It never falls in love

    Stops only ever go up. When a position is winning it locks in profit at 3x and 6x. If the creator sells, liquidity leaves or the trend breaks, it walks out without waiting.

    It moves money instead of waiting

    When the room turns defensive, money goes to cash. When it turns constructive, it goes back to work. A pocket that goes flat gives its budget to one that is moving.

    It never trades to make up a bad day

    A daily brake: after a bad enough day, Lloyd stops opening new positions until the next review. No revenge trades, no doubling down.

    It writes everything down

    Every buy, sell, rotation and every "did nothing" gets a note with what Lloyd knew at the time. You read the same feed we do, on the live book.

    It tests before it adopts

    Every new rule is run as an experiment first, with a question, a method and a verdict in the research ledger. Only what survives touches real money, and the book changes weekly because of it.

    Your money stays in your pocket

    Lloyd can buy and sell. It cannot withdraw, and it cannot send your funds anywhere but back to you.

    • Swaps only. Everything it buys lands back in your own bot wallet.
    • Your limit. You choose the biggest drop you accept. If it gets there, everything moves to USDC and Lloyd stops on your wallet.
    • Red button. Close everything at once, from your dashboard, with one in-page confirmation.
    • Nothing under the counter. Every move, with its reasoning, where you can see it.

    Before you pull up a stool

    You can lose

    Memecoins move hard in both directions. Lloyd does not guarantee profits, and no honest tool can. An edge means better odds than walking in blind, not a sure thing.

    It is still learning

    Lloyd is young and changes every week. Coming in early means trading with a system that is still finding its feet, and taking the risk that comes with that.

    Stops can slip

    In a sudden crash a sell set at −30% can fill lower. That is why the book spreads risk across pockets and positions, and why your limit is a target, not a guarantee.

    Expectations are estimates

    "What to expect" comes from simulating thousands of paths with Lloyd's own results. While real trades are few, those numbers are preliminary, and the widget says so.

    Shorting is not live yet

    In a falling market Lloyd leans on cash. Majors shorts through perps are being built; until they ship, defensive mostly means sitting in USDC.
