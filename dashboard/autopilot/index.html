What it does while you are not looking

We all lose money for the same reasons: we fall in love with a token, we skip the stop or move it, and we never have time to check who is behind it. The bot has none of those problems.

It checks who is behind it

Before buying, it traces who funded the top holders, whether the creator is a token factory, whether bundlers are in, and whether it can actually sell. If it smells like a rug, it stays out.

It moves its own stops

When a position is winning, it raises the stop to lock in profit. If the creator sells or liquidity leaves, it exits without waiting.

It keeps your money working

If a position goes flat and something better shows up, it rotates. If nothing is good enough, it holds cash and tells you why.

It learns from every mistake

Every trade is logged with what the bot knew when it entered. Every loss gets a cause and a cost, and new rules are tested before they touch your money.

Your money never leaves your wallet

The bot can buy and sell. Nothing else. It cannot withdraw or send funds anywhere, and you can take your money out or switch it off at any time.

  • Swaps onlyEverything it buys lands back in your wallet.
  • Your stopYou choose how much you are willing to lose. If it gets there, everything moves to USDC and the bot pauses.
  • Panic buttonSell everything on the spot, from your dashboard.
  • Nothing hiddenEvery buy and every sell, with its reasoning, in your dashboard.

    What you should know before you join

    You can lose. Memecoins move hard in both directions. The bot does not guarantee profits, and no honest tool can.

    It is still learning. The bot is new and changes every week. Joining early means joining a system that is still learning, with the risk that comes with it.

    Stops can slip. In a sudden crash, a sell set at −30% can fill lower. That is why the bot spreads risk across several positions.

    Odds are estimates. They come from simulating thousands of paths with the bot's results. While real trades are few, they are preliminary, and the page says so.
